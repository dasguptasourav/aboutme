Success Story #3Customer lifetime value, by product

Built a set of gradient boosting regressor models to predict customer lifetime value, split by product line rather than one model for everything: three core products shared a model, since their usage patterns generalised, while each of the other products needed its own. Features spanned product usage, account usage, the trend of both over time, pricing tier and firmographics, plus expansion and contraction signal. The main use case was paid search: feeding predicted lifetime value back into bid optimisation, rather than buying acquisition on last-click conversion alone.

Six feature groups feed gradient boosting regressors split by product line. Predicted lifetime value per account then sets how much each paid search click is worth.

Success Story #5Commercial & business performance insights

Most teams can tell leadership that ARR beat or missed forecast. I build the models that show exactly which lever moved, by how much, and what to do about it — down to the seat, the segment and the driver.

The starting point is always the same question: what actually happened to revenue this period, and why. That means decomposing closing ARR back to its seat-level drivers — new logos landed, cross-sell, expansion, contraction, reactivation and churn — and pricing it correctly across the tiers that carry the business. Below is the bridge I use to hold that decomposition together, distinguishing sales-led motion from product-led motion.

Bridge model: seat-level movement (sales-led and product-led) × price realised per paid seat by tier = closing ARR, split by go-to-market motion.

This bridge is the frame. The value is in what it surfaces when a period comes in off-plan:

  • Discounting leakageTraced a forecast miss to incorrect discount coding that was stacking across renewal cycles — not a demand problem, a billing rule problem.
  • Grace period gapMissed-payment accounts were coding straight to involuntary churn with no grace window. Quantified the ARR at risk and built the case to introduce one.
  • Land target missSegmentation showed the shortfall was concentrated in a single sales-led channel, not broad-based — changing the fix from a pricing review to a coverage review.

Built the business case on three numbers — productivity (one hour a week saved per analyst across roughly 300 analysts, near $1.5M a year), reusability (teams were duplicating the same definitions and SQL logic independently), and speed to insight (close to 10x faster from question asked to question answered). Took that case to my own manager first, stress-tested the harder, less certain cost side with him, and only then approached the five department heads and two platform-adjacent teams whose people and participation the plan actually needed.

Success Story #7One definition, one number, owned by name

Getting the platform right still leaves a governance problem: whose definition of a metric is the real one. This was cross-functional work, run with the departments that owned the metrics, with a named owner in each team for each definition and segment. It took roughly a year to get through the full set.

Agree the definitions
Metric by metric, segment by segment, worked through with the owning department until there was one accepted definition — not a data-team version and a finance version.
Assign ownership
Each definition sat with a named individual in the relevant team, so drift had someone accountable to catch it.
Embed it in reporting
Reports across departments converged on the same number for the same metric — the test of whether the governance had actually taken.
Compound the payoff
Consistent segmentation turned out to matter most in experimentation — A/B tests read the same segment the same way everywhere, so results were comparable across teams for the first time.

Success Story #8Build internal governance tools

With Platform Engineering, I designed and built a React.js-based metrics dictionary that became the single reference for every definition, used for both reporting and experimentation.

Success Story #9Coaching for data literacy

Alongside the governance work, ran hands-on coaching across visualisation practice, storytelling with data and self-service analytics — the standard was a team member who could turn a shared number into a decision unassisted, not just retrieve it.

“Early on, the question in every leadership review was which number is correct. Eighteen months in, that question had essentially disappeared — the conversation had moved to how do we tell this story better.”

Success Story #10Centre of Excellence

Before the Business Intelligence Centre of Excellence existed, best practice across analytics, BI and data engineering lived entirely in individual people's heads — no shared standard, so it left whenever they did. Built the team through lateral hires, contractor hires and internal staff moves, against one hiring bar: someone who could sit with a stakeholder directly and design and build their own solution, not wait for a spec.

Business Intelligence Centre of Excellence Framework
The BI Core Group sits at the centre, built on five pillars. Platform and foundation teams build the shared layers. Embedded analytics teams keep their reporting lines and draw best practice, support and mentorship from the hub.

Partnered directly with Data Engineering to build a centralised data model underneath the reporting layer — internally called the hypercube — a single governed source running from company-wide topline KPIs down to the smallest segment a given team needed, and with Platform Engineering on the governance tooling itself.

  • Metrics under governanceGrew from six to thirty-five over roughly eighteen months, expanding because teams pulled it in themselves rather than being mandated to.
  • Measured impactThe Centre of Excellence delivered over $1.5M in measurable productivity impact through the shared standards and tooling it built.
  • Career pipelinePulled junior team members into stakeholder conversations early, including sessions with the CEO and Exec Ops, so the bench built careers, not just technical depth.
